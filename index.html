Same success, different quality.
SPECTRA scores the quality of robotic manipulation with a Task Quality Index Q ∈ [0, 1].

How it works

Learn what demonstrations agree on. Penalize what a new execution doesn't.

Part 1

Constraint estimation

Demonstrations are aligned by task progress. Relative-pose directions that vary little across demonstrations are identified as constrained, and their spread defines the admissible variation.

Part 2

Policy evaluation

Deviations of an unseen execution along constrained directions are penalized only beyond the admissible variation. The mean cost and a terminal gate are combined into the Task Quality Index Q.

Object-centric, robot-agnostic, no human labels

  • Relative poses. Five pairs: the manipulated object in the reference's frame, and both objects relative to the gripper and the support.
  • Aligned by progress. Demos are split into sub-tasks (reach, grasp, carry, release) and resampled onto a shared progress axis.
  • No human labels. The reference comes from 30 successful demonstrations scripted in simulation.
  • Constrained where demos agree. At each step, any direction whose spread across 30 demos stays under 4 mm is constrained, and that spread becomes its tolerance.
